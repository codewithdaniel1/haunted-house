## The look

- **Place:** a coherent, lived-in Victorian-era mansion, with room proportions and architecture that connect across five levels: basement, floors 1–3, and attic.
- **Materials:** carved and worn wood, aged plaster, patterned wallpaper, tarnished brass, glass, stone, ironwork, rugs, fabric, and books. Keep object scale and contact with floors and tables believable.
- **Lighting:** cold moonlight and fog outside; warm practical lamps and candles inside. Each room needs a readable focal point, with darker corners that remain navigable. The flashlight should reveal material and clue detail.
- **Color:** charcoal and blue-gray shadows, muted burgundy and brown interiors, amber practical light, and pale cyan for rare supernatural events.
- **Mood:** believable rooms first; hauntings interrupt them. Use distance, darkness, subtle movement, and sound to build tension.
- **Composition:** tall doors, detailed ceilings, long halls, grounded furniture, and memorable silhouettes. Important clues should be visible when the player approaches and inspects them.
- **Story objects:** portraits, journals, clocks, music boxes, ritual tools, and mirrors should feel like belongings from one family and period.

## Staircases and doors

The central stair should orient the player and connect floors 1–3. Its floor 1 base is a solid, boxed-in stair with no old basement opening or surrounding guardrail. Floors 2 and 3 retain the cutouts and guards needed for the flights arriving from below. The basement and attic flights occupy different parts of the house. A service door at the south end of the floor 1 west hall opens into a short vestibule, then a plastered stair descends east toward the basement hall. A boarded, ward-marked door in the floor 3 east hall wall opens to an enclosed timber stair that rises north to the attic hall. Keep thresholds, solid risers, handholds, headroom, and floor transitions clear in first person.

Historic-house examples support enclosed secondary stairs and distinct attic access: [Historic England describes a panelled door fitted at the bottom of an enclosed stair](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/heritage-gateway/results/record/c30a9cd9-4500-41f0-acda-2ed4a4d72a681/MNA127358), and the [National Park Service documents a separate attic staircase at Lockwood House](https://www.nps.gov/media/photo/gallery-item.htm?gid=2D8FA290-EFD3-4049-8006-100347D105E1&id=5ce1183d-12b7-46c8-8481-8a99ac4e92e4&pg=7406854). These are architectural references, not assets to copy directly.

## How this applies to the game

The browser prototype contains connected rooms on all five levels, clue-driven locked doors and hidden spaces, three escape routes, and one fixed mode. Its materials, furniture, and room lighting are still an initial pass; improve them as playable geometry rather than using the screenshots as flat backgrounds. The game uses a flashlight and journal; ghost photography is not part of the current design.
